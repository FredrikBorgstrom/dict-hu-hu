# Exact-surface correction: fázókat

Prepared and published on 6 November 2026 to the existing production Hungarian
standard, classic and mixed dictionaries. Public artifact checksums and the
deployed reader on both serving origins verified acceptance of the exact form;
existing runtime removals were preserved.

Admit the exact lowercase surface `fázókat`, the accusative plural of
`fázó` used substantivally for people who feel cold. The existing vocabulary
already accepts `fázó`, `fázók`, `fázót` and `fázóknak`, but omits this form.
Its seven written letters are below the unchanged ten-character build limit.

The Research Centre for Linguistics' spelling adviser recognized the exact
surface without suggesting a correction on 6 November 2026:
<https://helyesiras.mta.hu/helyesiras/default/suggest?q=f%C3%A1z%C3%B3kat>.
This was checked through the backend's existing
`HungarianSpellingAdviserProvider`, independently of the player's screenshot
of a Google-generated answer about the different form `fázók`.

The current published mixed-tile graph was checked with the actual backend
reader and production orthography: it rejects `fázókat` while accepting the
four related forms above. The standard, classic and mixed local outputs
likewise omit the reported form. This establishes the exact coverage gap;
the preserved admission evidence identifies the original rejection as
`weak_derivation`: morphdb.hu recognizes a non-proper derived form, but the
cleanest corpus partition contains only one occurrence, below the existing
two-occurrence gate. Pinned Magyar Ispell independently supplies the expected
present-participle, plural and accusative analysis of `fázik` (English:
"to feel cold"). Retain that general gate and admit only this reviewed surface.

Record the correction in both the portable `gameplay_overrides.json` and
the parent `_community_overrides/hungarian_hu_hu_ispell/` decision register.
The normal evidence builder applies this exact reviewed addition on every
build. It does not license a new inflection family or change any reviewed
removal, alphabet, length limit or automated-player vocabulary.

All three human dictionaries can represent the spelling. Both tile modes
use exactly F | Á | Z | Ó | K | A | T; no digraph ambiguity or contraction
is involved. The artifact regression checks admission in the standard list
and the exact canonical key in both tile outputs.

The local candidate must differ from the preserved vocabulary only by this
surface. Preserve the current runtime additive/subtractive overlays for any
later publication, including removals approved after the source baseline.
Player correspondence and raw investigation evidence remain private.
